PETROBRAS (PETR4) — Earnings Call 4T24 / Q4 2024
Date: February 21, 2025
Participants:
  - Magda Chambriard, CEO
  - Fernando Melgarejo, CFO
  - Clarice Coppetti, Investor Relations Director
  - [Analysts from Itaú BBA, XP Investimentos, BTG Pactual, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley]

==============================================================================
OPENING REMARKS
==============================================================================

Clarice Coppetti (IR Director):
Good morning, everyone, and welcome to Petrobras' Fourth Quarter 2024 Earnings Conference Call.
Today we will present our results for the period, followed by a Q&A session. I'd like to remind
you that this call is being recorded and may contain forward-looking statements. I will now turn
the call over to our CEO, Dr. Magda Chambriard.

Magda Chambriard (CEO):
Thank you, Clarice. Good morning to all our investors and analysts. I am very proud to announce
that Petrobras delivered its strongest pre-salt production year on record in 2024, averaging
2.9 million barrels of oil equivalent per day in Q4 alone. This represents a 7% increase over
Q3 2024 and confirms our operational excellence in the Santos Basin.

We have continued to prioritize safety, efficiency, and returns. Our lifting cost remained at
$6.5 per barrel, which is one of the lowest in our peer group globally. This cost discipline,
combined with record production, translated into Adjusted EBITDA of R$ 67.4 billion for the
quarter, up from R$ 61.2 billion in Q3 2024.

Now, I want to address the question many of you have been asking: our dividend policy. As you
know, our 2024-2028 Strategic Plan committed to distributing at least 45% of free cash flow to
shareholders. I am pleased to confirm that we will maintain this commitment. For 2024 as a whole,
we will distribute R$ 72.9 billion in dividends, which represents an extraordinary payout ratio
of 68% of free cash flow. This reflects the Board's confidence in our cash generation capacity.

I also want to highlight a strategic decision that we are announcing today for the first time:
we have approved a new investment program for the Rota 3 gas pipeline expansion in Rio de Janeiro,
with a total capex of R$ 8.7 billion over 2025-2027. This was not included in our previous
strategic plan and reflects new commercial agreements with industrial clients in the Southeast
region. We expect this to add incremental EBITDA of approximately R$ 1.2 billion per year from 2027.

I will now pass the word to our CFO, Fernando Melgarejo, for the financial highlights.

Fernando Melgarejo (CFO):
Thank you, Magda. Good morning. Let me walk you through the key financial metrics for Q4 2024.

Net revenues reached R$ 145.3 billion, up 4% sequentially, primarily driven by higher production
volumes partially offset by a 3% decrease in realized Brent prices in R$ terms. Our lifting cost
came in at R$ 37.80 per barrel, which at the prevailing exchange rate represents approximately
USD 6.5 per barrel, consistent with our guidance.

Operating cash flow was R$ 53.1 billion in Q4, and we ended the year with gross debt of
R$ 312.8 billion and net debt of R$ 205.6 billion. Our net debt-to-EBITDA ratio stands at
0.76x, comfortably below our strategic ceiling of 2.0x.

Regarding 2025 capex guidance: we are revising our 2025 investment plan upward to R$ 104 billion,
from the previously communicated R$ 89 billion. This increase of approximately R$ 15 billion
reflects three factors: first, the Rota 3 expansion announced by Magda; second, acceleration of
our Buzios Phase 7 development following the successful FPSO commissioning; and third, higher
anticipated costs related to our refinery revamp program at RNEST. We are confident this
accelerated investment will deliver superior returns in the 2026-2028 window.

Let me also address our production guidance for 2025. We are maintaining our production target of
2.8 to 3.0 million barrels of oil equivalent per day. Some analysts have modeled higher production
following Q4's strong performance. I want to be careful not to get ahead of ourselves here —
there are still uncertainties around FPSO scheduling that prevent us from revising upward at
this stage.

==============================================================================
Q&A SESSION
==============================================================================

Clarice Coppetti (IR):
We will now open the Q&A session. Please limit questions to two per analyst. Our first question
comes from Gabriel Barra at Itaú BBA.

Gabriel Barra (Itaú BBA):
Good morning, Magda, Fernando. Congratulations on the strong results. I have two questions.
First, on the dividend: you mentioned maintaining the 45% minimum, but the extraordinary payout
this year at 68% was driven by very specific cash dynamics. Can you commit to a similar above-minimum
payout for 2025, or should the market model closer to the 45% floor? And second, on capex,
the R$ 15 billion increase is quite significant relative to what was in the plan — was this
fully approved by the Board, and does it change the return hurdle for new projects?

Fernando Melgarejo (CFO):
Gabriel, thank you for the question. On dividends — we remain committed to the 45% minimum as
stated in our strategic plan. Regarding extraordinary distributions above the floor, this depends
on factors including our cash position, investment needs, and market conditions at the time of each
Board meeting. It would be premature for me to comment at this point on what 2025 extraordinary
dividends might look like. What I can say is that our cash generation remains robust and we are
well-positioned to continue rewarding shareholders. The capital structure is healthy.

On the capex revision: yes, the R$ 15 billion increase was approved by the Board in our January 2025
board meeting. Each of these projects clears our internal hurdle rate of 15% IRR at $70 Brent.
I want to make very clear — we are not sacrificing returns for growth.

[Analyst follow-up was cut off]

Clarice Coppetti (IR):
Our next question comes from Regis Cardoso at XP Investimentos.

Regis Cardoso (XP Investimentos):
Morning, everyone. My question is about the RNEST refinery. You mentioned higher costs in the
revamp program. Can you give us more color on what's driving that? There have been reports in
the press about contractor issues and delays. Is the R$ 4.2 billion additional capex at RNEST
fully within contingency budgets, or is this a new cost overrun? And separately, what's the
timeline for RNEST to reach full nameplate capacity?

Magda Chambriard (CEO):
Regis, I want to be direct with you. The RNEST situation is — look, it's a very complex project.
We've had some, let's say, challenges with one of our primary EPC contractors. We are working through
those challenges. What I can tell you is that we are committed to bringing RNEST to full capacity,
and we expect to provide a more detailed update in our Q1 2025 results call in May. At this stage,
I think it's important that we — let me think — we need to be careful about saying too much before
we have full clarity on the contractor situation. What I can say is that the overall project
economics remain attractive. Fernando, do you want to add something on the cost side?

Fernando Melgarejo (CFO):
Yes. The additional costs at RNEST are within — they are being evaluated within the revised budget
framework. I don't want to give you a specific number today because the contractor renegotiations
are ongoing. We will be transparent when we have finalized those negotiations. What I can confirm
is that the R$ 104 billion total capex guidance includes a contingency provision for RNEST,
and we are comfortable with that overall number.

Regis Cardoso (XP Investimentos):
Just to be clear — you cannot confirm whether RNEST is within the original contingency envelope
or represents a cost overrun beyond that?

Magda Chambriard (CEO):
What we can confirm is that the overall 2025 capex guidance of R$ 104 billion fully contemplates
our current expectation for RNEST expenditures. I think that's the most important data point.
Let's move forward.

Clarice Coppetti (IR):
Our next question comes from Bruno Amorim at Goldman Sachs.

Bruno Amorim (Goldman Sachs):
Good morning. My question is about pre-salt production costs. You maintained the lifting cost at
$6.5 per barrel, which is outstanding. But as you move into deeper, more complex Buzios phases —
Phase 7 and beyond — is there an inflection point where lifting costs start rising? And related to
that, how sensitive is your dividend capacity to a scenario where Brent drops to $65 per barrel
for an extended period?

Fernando Melgarejo (CFO):
Bruno, excellent questions. On lifting cost: we are actually targeting to keep lifting costs
below $7 per barrel through 2028, even as we develop deeper Buzios phases. This is achievable
through standardization of our FPSO design, optimization of our integrated operations model,
and continued digitalization of our maintenance programs. The cost trajectory is under control.

On Brent sensitivity: at $65 Brent, our cash generation remains positive and we would still be
able to meet our minimum 45% distribution commitment. However, I want to emphasize that below
$60 Brent, we would review all capital allocation priorities in light of the macroeconomic
scenario. We have stress-tested our balance sheet extensively, and I am confident in its
resilience.

Bruno Amorim (Goldman Sachs):
Can you be more specific on the free cash flow breakeven price? At what Brent level does free
cash flow after capex and distributions go to zero?

Fernando Melgarejo (CFO):
Bruno, that's a more granular sensitivity that we prefer to discuss in bilateral meetings with
investors. But I can say directionally that our breakeven — including capex and the minimum
distribution — is below $50 Brent. We're in a very comfortable position.

Clarice Coppetti (IR):
Our next question comes from Christian Audi at Morgan Stanley.

Christian Audi (Morgan Stanley):
Thank you. Two quick ones. First, on the Rota 3 gas pipeline — this is a material new announcement.
Can you share who the industrial offtakers are and what type of contract structure these are
take-or-pay or spot? And second, on ESG: you mentioned operational excellence but I noticed you
removed the Scope 3 emissions reduction target from your updated strategic plan. Can you explain
the rationale?

Magda Chambriard (CEO):
Christian, on Rota 3: the commercial agreements are with major industrial groups in the Southeast.
I cannot disclose the counterparties at this stage as the contracts are not yet publicly filed.
What I can confirm is that these are predominantly take-or-pay structures with a weighted average
contract duration of 15 years. This gives us very high revenue visibility.

On Scope 3 — I want to be direct. We made a deliberate decision to focus our emissions reduction
commitments on areas where we have operational control, which is Scopes 1 and 2. Scope 3 involves
our customers' emissions, which are outside our direct management. We believe our commitments on
Scope 1 and 2 are among the most ambitious in the industry and represent a credible, achievable
pathway. We are not stepping back from climate commitments — we are being more rigorous about
what we can actually deliver.

Clarice Coppetti (IR):
We have time for one final question. Leonardo Marcondes from BTG Pactual.

Leonardo Marcondes (BTG Pactual):
Morning. On the extraordinary dividend — you paid R$ 72.9 billion in 2024 versus R$ 63.1 billion
in 2023. The market had broadly expected around R$ 70 billion, so this is slightly above consensus.
But looking at 2025, with capex up R$ 15 billion, wouldn't the math suggest a significantly lower
total distribution? I'm trying to reconcile the capex increase with shareholder returns messaging.

Fernando Melgarejo (CFO):
Leonardo, you're right that there is a mechanical impact from higher capex on free cash flow.
However, I want to highlight two things: first, our operating cash flow is also expected to
grow as production ramps up in 2025; and second, the Rota 3 and Buzios Phase 7 investments
begin generating returns within the strategic plan horizon. We remain committed to the 45% floor.
Beyond that, as I said to Gabriel, the Board will evaluate extraordinary distributions as we
go through the year. I don't want to set a specific number for 2025 distributions at this time.

Magda Chambriard (CEO):
Let me close by reaffirming that Petrobras is in an exceptional position: record production,
best-in-class costs, a healthy balance sheet, and a clear strategic direction. We are investing
for the future without sacrificing the present. Thank you all for joining us today.

Clarice Coppetti (IR):
Thank you all. A replay of this call will be available on our IR website. Have a good day.

[END OF CALL]
